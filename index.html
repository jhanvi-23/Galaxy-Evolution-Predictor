01 / THE PROJECT

Turning observations into predictions.

A data-driven study using the KiDS DR4 galaxy catalogue. We train two separate regression models to reproduce catalogue estimates of two important galaxy properties.

A universe written in numbers.

Astronomical surveys capture light from enormous populations of distant galaxies. Their measured photometric properties contain patterns that machine learning models can learn.

Our workflow uses nine GAaP magnitude bands, their nine associated magnitude errors, and GaZNet redshift as input features. Two independent models estimate stellar mass and star-formation rate.

The goal is to explore how effectively these catalogue values can be reproduced from the selected observations.

MODEL A / REGRESSION

Stellar mass model

A HistGradientBoostingRegressor learns relationships between the selected input features and the catalogue's stellar-mass estimate.

  • Target: MASS_MED_GaZNet
  • Gradient-boosted decision trees
  • Evaluated on held-out test data
MODEL B / REGRESSION

Star-formation model

A separate HistGradientBoostingRegressor learns patterns associated with the catalogue's star-formation-rate estimate.

  • Target: SFR_MED_GaZNet
  • Independent model training
  • Evaluated on held-out test data
COSMIC CONTEXT / BEYOND THE MODEL

The invisible architecture of the universe.

Galaxies are commonly studied in the context of dark matter halos: vast, invisible structures whose gravity influences how galaxies form and evolve. Their study is an important part of modern cosmology.

This animated halo is a conceptual scientific illustration. Dark matter, halo mass and gravitational lensing are not direct prediction outputs of this project's two ML models.
